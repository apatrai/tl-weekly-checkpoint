Retention & pricing: the two strategic questions from the 27.07 weekly

WHY NOW
Growth is currently blocked by two gaps (Anton + Ishay weekly, 27.07):
trial-to-paid conversion and retention. Context:
- New paid seats keep falling, while WhatsApp-connected volume did not fall
  proportionally — the leak is conversion and retention, not top-of-funnel.
- Deals got small: big custom-discount deals (100+ seats, used to be 1–2 a
  week) disappeared; recent deals skew 5–20 seats.
- We recently lost a serious mid-market client; MRR is near-flat — right now
  "small growth" mostly means "absence of loss".
- Outside view (HelpScout founder, via Ishay): for SMB/mid-market, message
  sync with the CRM may be an ACTIVATION value — easy to get, and once
  absorbed, easy to leave. Retention likely needs value on top of the sync
  itself. His pricing point: a churned user saying "too expensive" at an
  unchanged price really means perceived value dropped; a single per-seat
  metric has stopped working for many SaaS over the last half-year.

APPENDIX — REVFIXR'S DIAGNOSIS OF US (approach summary)
RevFixr is a SaaS pricing consultancy (Amsterdam) we evaluated in Jun–Jul
2026. Their read on us — largely confirmed by our own data:
- Net revenue retention is below 100% (~96%/mo blended): the base is in net
  contraction — seat expansion does not outrun churn + contraction.
- Inverted retention: top-tier customers decline while bottom-tier grows;
  the top MRR quartile is ~69% of revenue with the worst (and falling)
  retention.
- No monetization-escalation levers: nothing that charges heavier or more
  complex users more (no triggers, caps, limits).
- Volume discounting is backwards: bigger accounts pay LESS per seat while
  getting more value; all discounting is squeezed onto the one seat
  dimension.
- Seat-only pricing "cost-stacks" against the CRM: buyers mentally add our
  seat price to HubSpot's seat price instead of judging our own value.
- Their thesis: selective monetization of larger accounts (upgrade ladder,
  add-ons, gated compliance) rather than equal per-seat treatment.
Their method: design packaging first, then validate deal-by-deal on
custom-quote deals BEFORE touching the public pricing page (works from as
few as ~5 sales conversations).
Public case they cite: Geogap — segment-based plans → ARPU +65%, MRR +47%,
churn −11% (https://revfixr.com/geogap). Pricing pages they point to as
models: Avoma, Basecamp, GoHighLevel, Fireflies.ai (seat-tier escalation).
Internal caveat to keep in mind: their playbook leans enterprise, while 54%
of our customers have exactly 1 seat and 69% have ≤2 — treat this as input
for your own judgement on questions 1–2, not as the answer.
